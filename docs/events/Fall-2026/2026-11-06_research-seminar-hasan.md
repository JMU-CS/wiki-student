## Abstract

Wireless networks do much more than carry information from one device to another. The signals traveling through the air also reveal information about the devices transmitting them, the surrounding environment, and even human activity. At the same time, as AI becomes increasingly integrated into wireless systems, new questions arise about how these networks make decisions and whether those decisions can be trusted.

In this talk, I will explore three connected directions from my research, viewing the wireless channel as a resource, a sensor, and a source of physical evidence. First, I will discuss how AI can improve wireless resource allocation while respecting practical constraints such as interference and quality of service. Next, I will show how radio frequency (RF) fingerprinting and Wi-Fi sensing can provide information about device identity and human motion, with potential applications in authentication and secure video conferencing. Finally, I will examine security challenges in distributed learning and inference, focusing on how malicious participants can exploit wireless channel variations to manipulate intelligent systems while avoiding detection.

Drawing on recent research findings and ongoing projects, I will discuss both the opportunities and limitations of these approaches, along with some of the open problems we are investigating. The broader goal of my talk would be to understand how we can build wireless systems that are not only more intelligent and adaptive, but also more secure and trustworthy.
